# Gas Leak and Emergency Response Procedure
Document GS-EM-101 · Revision 7 · Owner: Gas Emergency Operations
FICTIONAL TRAINING SAMPLE — not an actual SDG&E document.

## 1. Purpose and Scope
This procedure defines how field and office employees respond to reports of gas odor, suspected leaks, damaged facilities, fire, or explosion involving natural gas facilities. It applies to all Gas Operations employees, contractors working under company supervision, and Customer Service Field (CSF) technicians. Public and employee safety take priority over property, service continuity, and schedule in every situation.

## 2. Immediate Actions on Arrival — "Make Safe First"
When responding to a reported gas odor or leak, the first employee on scene must:
1. Park upwind and at least 100 feet from the reported location. Do not drive into a visible vapor cloud or an area of strong odor.
2. Approach on foot from upwind. Carry a combustible gas indicator (CGI) that has been bump-tested within the last 24 hours.
3. Eliminate ignition sources: do not operate electrical switches, doorbells, garage door openers, or vehicles inside the hazard area. Do not use a cell phone or radio within 25 feet of a suspected gas concentration.
4. Evacuate occupants of affected structures when any CGI reading inside a structure is at or above 10% of the Lower Explosive Limit (LEL), or when odor is strong and the source is unknown. Direct evacuees upwind to a safe assembly point at least 300 feet away.
5. Call 911 and notify Gas Emergency Dispatch at extension 4-GAS (4-427) whenever there is evacuation, fire, explosion, injury, or blowing gas.
6. Establish a perimeter and keep the public out. Request police and fire support for traffic and crowd control.

Never attempt to locate a leak inside a structure by using an open flame. Never re-enter an evacuated structure until readings are below 1% LEL throughout and the source has been isolated.

## 3. Leak Grading
All leaks discovered must be graded using the company leak classification:
- Grade 1 — A leak that represents an existing or probable hazard to persons or property and requires immediate repair or continuous action until conditions are no longer hazardous. Examples: any reading inside or under a building; gas migrating toward a building; any leak that can be seen, heard, or felt where it may endanger the public; escaping gas that has ignited.
- Grade 2 — A leak that is non-hazardous at the time of detection but justifies scheduled repair based on probable future hazard. Grade 2 leaks must be repaired or cleared within 15 months of the date reported and re-evaluated at least once every six months until cleared.
- Grade 3 — A leak that is non-hazardous at the time of detection and can reasonably be expected to remain non-hazardous. Grade 3 leaks must be re-evaluated during the next scheduled survey or within 15 months of the date reported, whichever occurs first.

A Grade 1 leak may never be left unattended. The responding crew remains on site until the hazard is eliminated by repair, isolation, or venting and readings confirm safe conditions.

## 4. Shutting Off Gas
Customer service valves (meter valves) may be closed by any qualified employee to make an area safe. Closing a distribution main valve or regulator station valve requires authorization from Gas Control (extension 4-4100) because it can interrupt service to many customers and create low-pressure conditions downstream. Document the time, valve number, and the name of the Gas Control operator who authorized the operation.

After an emergency shut-off, service must only be restored by a qualified technician who performs a leak check, purges the customer piping, and relights every appliance pilot. Never restore service to a premise where you cannot gain access to relight appliances; leave a red "Gas Off" tag on the meter instead.

## 5. Excavation Damage ("Dig-In") Response
If a third party damages a gas line:
1. Instruct the excavator to shut off equipment and move personnel away. Do not attempt to crimp or plug a blowing plastic line by hand — static discharge can ignite the gas.
2. Evacuate the area within at least 300 feet downwind for blowing gas on mains of 2 inches or larger.
3. Notify Gas Emergency Dispatch, who will dispatch a crew equipped to squeeze off the line upstream and downstream of the damage.
4. Photograph the damage, the excavator's equipment, and any locate marks or their absence. Record the USA North/DigAlert ticket number.
5. Complete the Damage Incident Report (form GS-114) before the end of the shift.

## 6. Reporting Requirements
Gas Emergency Dispatch and the on-call Supervisor must be notified immediately of any incident involving a release of gas together with a death, an injury requiring in-patient hospitalization, estimated property damage of $50,000 or more (including cost of gas lost), an unintentional estimated gas loss of three million cubic feet or more, or significant media attention. The Compliance team makes the required regulatory notifications to PHMSA and the CPUC; field employees must provide time of discovery, location, and conditions as quickly as possible because federal telephonic notice is due no later than one hour after confirmed discovery. See the Regulatory Compliance Guide (GS-RC-300) for full details.

## 7. Communication With the Public
Only the Incident Commander or Corporate Communications may make statements to the media. Employees should politely direct reporters to the Corporate Communications hotline. Be courteous to customers, explain that the action is being taken for their safety, and provide the 24-hour customer line for follow-up.

## 8. Post-Incident
Within 24 hours the supervisor must complete an incident review, collect CGI calibration and bump-test records, preserve any failed material for laboratory analysis (do not discard fittings or pipe sections), and schedule a crew debrief. Near-misses are reported through the Safety Observation system and are never grounds for discipline when reported in good faith.
