# Distribution System Maintenance Protocols
Document GS-MT-150 · Revision 9 · Owner: Gas Distribution Engineering
FICTIONAL TRAINING SAMPLE — not an actual SDG&E document.

## 1. Scope
These protocols cover recurring maintenance and inspection of the gas distribution system: mains, services, district regulator stations, valves, meter sets, and cathodic protection systems. Intervals listed are the company's internal standards, which equal or exceed the minimum federal requirements of 49 CFR Part 192.

## 2. Leak Surveys
- Business districts: leak survey at intervals not exceeding 15 months, but at least once each calendar year, using a flame ionization or laser methane detector, including tests of the atmosphere in gas, electric, telephone, sewer and water manholes, at cracks in pavement and sidewalks, and at other locations providing an opportunity for finding gas leaks.
- Outside business districts: leak survey at least once every 5 calendar years at intervals not exceeding 63 months. Company standard: unprotected steel and cast-iron segments are surveyed every 3 years.
- Special surveys are performed after earthquakes of magnitude 5.0 or greater within 50 miles of company facilities, after blasting, and before paving projects over existing mains.

## 3. Cathodic Protection (CP)
- Pipe-to-soil potential surveys for cathodically protected pipelines: at least once each calendar year, at intervals not exceeding 15 months. The protection criterion is a negative (cathodic) potential of at least -850 mV with reference to a saturated copper-copper sulfate electrode, with IR drop considered.
- Rectifiers: inspect six times each calendar year, at intervals not exceeding 2½ months.
- Isolated short sections and services protected by galvanic anodes may be sampled at 10% per year so that the entire system is tested within each 10-year period.
- Any reading less negative than -850 mV must be reported to Corrosion Engineering within 5 business days, and remedial action must be scheduled within 90 days.

## 4. Valve Maintenance
Each valve that may be necessary for the safe operation of the distribution system ("emergency valve") must be checked and serviced at intervals not exceeding 15 months, but at least once each calendar year. Servicing includes locating the valve box, cleaning it out, verifying the valve is accessible, partially operating the valve, and lubricating as specified by the manufacturer. Inoperable emergency valves must be reported to Gas Control immediately and repaired or replaced within 30 days; an alternate isolation plan must be documented in the meantime.

## 5. District Regulator Stations
Each pressure-limiting and regulating station must be inspected and tested at intervals not exceeding 15 months, but at least once each calendar year, to verify it is in good mechanical condition, adequate from the standpoint of capacity and reliability, set to control or relieve at the correct pressure, and properly installed and protected from dirt, liquids, or other conditions that might prevent proper operation. Relief valve capacity must be reviewed whenever station equipment or downstream load changes. Record set points, lock-up pressures, and as-found/as-left readings on form GS-152.

## 6. Purging Procedures for New or Replaced Piping
1. Purging must be planned with a written purge plan for any main 4 inches in diameter or larger.
2. When purging air out of new piping with gas, introduce gas at a moderately rapid and continuous rate to prevent a hazardous mixture of gas and air. Where this is not practical, place a slug of inert gas (nitrogen) ahead of the natural gas.
3. Vent purge gas through a riser at least 8 feet above grade, away from buildings, overhead lines, and ignition sources, with a bonded and grounded vent stack.
4. Purging is complete when a combustible gas indicator reads at least 90% gas by volume for three consecutive readings taken one minute apart at the vent outlet.
5. For service lines, purge at the meter riser outlet and confirm gas at the riser before setting the meter. Never purge into a structure.

## 7. Pressure Testing
New mains and services must be pressure tested before being placed in service. Plastic pipe operating at or below 100 psig: test at 150% of maximum operating pressure or 50 psig, whichever is greater, but not exceeding three times the design pressure, for a minimum of 1 hour (company standard: 4 hours for mains). Steel service lines: minimum 90 psig for 10 minutes when operated at 1 psig or more. Record the test medium, pressure, duration, temperature, and the name of the qualified person conducting the test.

## 8. Operator Qualification (OQ)
Only employees with a current Operator Qualification for the specific covered task may perform it, or a non-qualified individual may perform it under the direct observation of a qualified individual (span of control 1:1 for abnormal-operating-condition-sensitive tasks). OQ status is checked in the Learning Management System before dispatch. Requalification intervals are 3 years for most tasks and 1 year for tapping and stopping of mains.

## 9. Records Retention
Maintenance records must be entered into the Work Management System within 5 business days of completion. Leak survey and CP records are retained for at least 5 years; pressure test records for the life of the facility.
