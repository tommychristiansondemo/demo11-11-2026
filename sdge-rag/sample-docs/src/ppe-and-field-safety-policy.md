# Personal Protective Equipment and Field Safety Policy
Document GS-SF-210 · Revision 4 · Owner: Safety, Health & Environment (SH&E)
FICTIONAL TRAINING SAMPLE — not an actual SDG&E document.

## 1. Policy Statement
Every employee has the right and the responsibility to stop work that they believe is unsafe ("Stop Work Authority"). No job is so urgent that it cannot be done safely. Supervisors must support any employee who exercises Stop Work Authority in good faith, and the work may resume only after the hazard is evaluated and controlled.

## 2. Minimum PPE for All Field Work
The following PPE is required at all times in the field, including yards and job sites:
- ANSI Z89.1 Type I Class E hard hat
- ANSI Z87.1 safety glasses with side shields
- ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 high-visibility vest or garment (Class 3 when working in or adjacent to traffic lanes at night or on roadways with posted speed limits above 45 mph)
- Leather safety-toe boots meeting ASTM F2413, at least 6 inches high
- Work gloves appropriate to the task (cut-resistant ANSI A4 or higher for handling pipe and fittings)

## 3. Flame-Resistant (FR) Clothing
FR clothing with an arc thermal performance value (ATPV) of at least 8 cal/cm² is required for any work on or within 10 feet of live gas facilities, including meter set work, tapping, squeezing off, purging, and leak investigation. FR garments must be worn as the outer layer, sleeves rolled down and fastened. Synthetic base layers (polyester, nylon) are prohibited under FR clothing because they melt; cotton, wool, or FR-rated base layers are acceptable. Damaged or contaminated FR clothing must be replaced, not repaired with non-FR material.

## 4. Additional PPE for Excavation Near Gas Facilities
When excavating within 24 inches of a marked gas main or service (the "tolerance zone"):
- Hand dig or use vacuum excavation only; no mechanized digging inside the tolerance zone.
- Wear FR clothing as described above and face shield over safety glasses when exposing a pressurized facility.
- A combustible gas indicator must be monitored continuously in any excavation where gas odor is present or a leak is suspected.
- Excavations 5 feet deep or more require a protective system (sloping, shoring, or trench box) designed per Cal/OSHA Title 8 Article 6, and a competent person must inspect the excavation daily and after rain.
- A ladder or ramp must be within 25 feet of lateral travel for any trench 4 feet deep or more.

## 5. Respiratory Protection and Confined Spaces
Vaults and regulator pits are treated as permit-required confined spaces until atmospheric testing proves otherwise. Before entry, test in this order: oxygen (19.5%–23.5%), flammability (less than 10% LEL), then toxics (H2S below 10 ppm, CO below 25 ppm). Continuous monitoring is required during occupancy. An attendant must remain outside the space for the full duration of entry and must never enter to attempt a rescue; call 911 and use the retrieval system instead. Employees using respirators must be medically cleared and fit-tested annually.

## 6. Hearing Protection
Hearing protection (NRR 25 or higher) is required when operating or working within 25 feet of jackhammers, compressors, saw cutting, or blowing gas during purging and blowdowns.

## 7. Heat Illness Prevention
From May 1 through October 31, crews must carry at least one quart of water per employee per hour of the planned shift, and shade must be available when temperatures exceed 80°F. When temperatures reach 95°F or above, high-heat procedures apply: mandatory 10-minute cool-down rest every two hours, a designated observer for every 20 employees, and pre-shift meetings that review heat illness symptoms. New employees are closely observed during their first 14 days of work (acclimatization).

## 8. Driving and Vehicle Safety
- Hand-held cell phone use while driving any company vehicle is prohibited, including at stop lights. Hands-free calls are permitted only when necessary and brief; texting, email, and app use while driving are prohibited at all times.
- Perform a 360-degree walk-around before moving any vehicle. Use a spotter when backing large trucks whenever one is available.
- Seat belts are required for all occupants. Vehicles must carry a fire extinguisher (minimum 2A:10BC) and a first-aid kit.

## 9. Incident and Injury Reporting
All injuries, no matter how minor, must be reported to the supervisor before the end of the shift and entered in the SH&E reporting system within 24 hours. Vehicle incidents must be reported immediately. Employees may also report hazards anonymously through the Safety Hotline.
